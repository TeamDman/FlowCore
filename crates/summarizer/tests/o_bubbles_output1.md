Soap bubbles appear to have rainbow colors because of the **interference of reflected light** from the transparent water film on the surface. Here's how it works:

1. **Refraction and Reflection**: When light enters a soap bubble, it undergoes two key processes:  
   - **Refraction** at the air-water interface, causing different wavelengths of light to bend slightly.  
   - **Reflection** at the water-air interface, where the light reflects off the top and bottom surfaces.  

2. **Interference**: The reflected light from the top and bottom surfaces of the film causes constructive and destructive interference.  
   - **Constructive interference** (bright colors) occurs when the path difference between the reflected waves is an integer multiple of the wavelength.  
   - **Destructive interference** (dark colors) occurs when the path difference is a half-integer multiple of the wavelength.  

3. **Visible Spectrum**: The combination of these effects results in a spectrum of colors, which are visible due to the specific wavelengths of light that interact constructively.

In essence, the soap bubble's thin film acts like a prism, refracting and interfering the light to produce the rainbow effect. This is analogous to how rainbows occur in nature, but with a more "visible" and "thinner" prism.
