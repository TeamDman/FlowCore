Soap bubbles exhibit rainbow colors due to **thin film interference**, a phenomenon caused by the interaction of light waves reflecting off the top and bottom surfaces of the bubble's thin film (a layer of water with soap). Here's a breakdown of the key principles:

### 1. **Thin Film Interference**
   - Light reflects off the **top surface** (air-water interface) and the **bottom surface** (water-air interface) of the soap film.
   - The **path difference** between the two reflected waves is **2t**, where **t** is the thickness of the film.
   - The **refractive index** of the film (soap/water) affects the wavelength of light, as the speed of light in the film is reduced.

### 2. **Phase Shifts**
   - **Top reflection**: Light reflects off air (n=1) and **does not** experience a phase shift.
   - **Bottom reflection**: Light reflects off water (n=1.33) and **does experience** a **phase shift of π** (half a wavelength), effectively **inverting** the wave's phase.

### 3. **Constructive and Destructive Interference**
   - The total phase difference between the two reflected waves is:
     $$
     \Delta \phi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} \cdot 2t + \pi
     $$
   - For **constructive interference** (bright colors), the phase difference must be an **even multiple of π**:
     $$
     \frac{4\pi t}{\lambda} + \pi = 2\pi k \quad \Rightarrow \quad 4t = (2k - 1)\lambda
     $$
   - For **destructive interference** (dark colors), the phase difference must be an **odd multiple of π**:
     $$
     \frac{4\pi t}{\lambda} + \pi = (2k + 1)\pi \quad \Rightarrow \quad 4t = 2k\lambda
     $$

### 4. **Visible Colors**
   - The thickness of the soap film (≈100 nm) is such that **different wavelengths** of light (visible spectrum) satisfy the above conditions.
   - For example, when the thickness is approximately **λ/4**, constructive interference occurs for **red** light (longer wavelength), and destructive interference for **blue** (shorter wavelength).
   - This leads to the **rainbow effect**, where colors appear due to the varying thickness of the film and the interference of light waves.

### 5. **Why the Rainbow?**
   - The **varying thickness** of the soap film causes **different wavelengths** to interfere constructively or destructively, resulting in the **rainbow colors** visible when viewing the bubble.

### Summary
Soap bubbles display rainbow colors due to **thin film interference** caused by light reflecting off the top and bottom surfaces of the film. The interference depends on the film's thickness, refractive index, and the wavelength of light, leading to constructive and destructive interference for different colors. This phenomenon is a classic example of how light interacts with thin layers of material to produce colorful effects.
