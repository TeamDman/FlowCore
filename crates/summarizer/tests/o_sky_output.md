The sky appears blue due to **Rayleigh scattering**, a physical phenomenon where light interacts with the small particles and molecules in the Earth's atmosphere. Here's a breakdown of the key points:

### 1. **Sunlight and Wavelengths**  
   - The Sun emits light across a spectrum of wavelengths, from **violet (shortest) to red (longest)**.  
   - However, **our eyes are more sensitive to blue light** than violet, and the human eye's sensitivity to blue light makes it the dominant color perceived in the sky.

### 2. **Rayleigh Scattering**  
   - When sunlight enters the atmosphere, it collides with **gas molecules (like nitrogen and oxygen)** and **tiny particles** (like dust and aerosols).  
   - Shorter wavelengths (like violet and blue) are scattered more efficiently than longer wavelengths (like red and orange).  
   - This scattering redistributes the light, with blue light spreading more uniformly in all directions.

### 3. **Why Blue?**  
   - The scattered blue light reaches our eyes, creating the **overall blue appearance** of the sky.  
   - The violet light, which is scattered even more, is absorbed by the atmosphere or not perceived due to our eyes' sensitivity to it.

### 4. **Time of Day and Color**  
   - At **sunrise or sunset**, the light travels through a thicker layer of the atmosphere, allowing longer wavelengths (red, orange) to dominate.  
   - During the **day**, shorter wavelengths (blue) are scattered more, making the sky appear blue.

### 5. **Key Factors**  
   - **Atmospheric composition**: Nitrogen and oxygen molecules are the primary scatterers.  
   - **Angle of sunlight**: The path length through the atmosphere varies, affecting the intensity of scattered light.

### Summary  
The sky is blue because shorter wavelengths (blue and violet) are scattered more by atmospheric gases, and our eyes are tuned to detect blue light. This scattering effect creates the vivid blue sky we see, while longer wavelengths (red, orange) dominate at sunrise/sunset.
