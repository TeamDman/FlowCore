The sky appears blue due to **Rayleigh scattering**, a phenomenon where light interacts with the atmosphere. Here's a clear breakdown:

1. **Light and Wavelengths**:  
   Light is composed of different colors, each with unique wavelengths. Shorter wavelengths (like blue and violet) scatter more than longer wavelengths (like red and orange) when encountering particles in the atmosphere.

2. **Rayleigh Scattering**:  
   The scattering effect is proportional to the **fourth power of the wavelength**. This means blue and violet light, being shorter, scatter more in the atmosphere. However, the human eye is more sensitive to blue light, and the atmosphere absorbs some of the violet light, making it less visible.

3. **Why Blue?**  
   While both blue and violet are scattered, the **visible spectrum** we perceive is dominated by blue. Additionally, the atmosphere absorbs most of the violet light, so we don’t notice it.

4. **Sunlight and Scattering**:  
   During the day, sunlight passes through the atmosphere, and the blue light is scattered in all directions, creating the blue sky. This is why the sky appears blue, not violet, even though violet is scattered more.

5. **Sunrise/Sunset Effects**:  
   At sunrise or sunset, sunlight travels through a thicker layer of the atmosphere, allowing longer wavelengths (red and orange) to dominate. This is why the sky appears red during these times.

The sky is blue because shorter wavelengths (blue and violet) scatter more in the atmosphere due to Rayleigh scattering.
